How it works

Three things a spreadsheet cannot do

The list is the easy part. What breaks is everything the list does not know: who owns the date, what happened last time, and whether anyone has been told yet.

One list, every commitment

Contracts, certifications, insurance, security reviews and anything else with a date on it. Import a CSV or forward the confirmation email and Detent reads the date out of it.

A name against every date

Every commitment has one owner and one backup. When somebody leaves, their dates move to their manager rather than going quiet until the renewal lands.

Notice while it still matters

Set the lead time per commitment. A 90 day notice period gets raised at 120 days, in the channel the owner already reads, with last year's terms attached.

Questions

Before you start a trial

How is this different from a shared calendar?

A calendar tells you a date arrived. Detent tells you a decision is due, who owns it, what was agreed last time, and whether anything has happened since the reminder went out. The date is the smallest part of the record.

Can we import what we already have?

Yes. CSV import on every plan, and a mapping step that runs against your own column names. Most teams are importing within the first hour and correcting owners for a week after that.

What happens when the owner leaves the company?

On Team and Enterprise their commitments move to the named backup, and the manager gets a list of everything that moved. On Starter the commitments stay in place and are flagged as unowned, which is visible on the main list rather than hidden in a report.

Do you support SSO?

SAML and directory sync are on Enterprise. Everything below that is email and password with optional two factor authentication.

How does the annual discount work?

Paying yearly costs the same as ten months. Switch at any point and the change applies from your next invoice, with the unused part of the current period credited.
